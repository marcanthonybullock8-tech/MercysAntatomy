# Mercys Anatomy
## Season 2, Episode 1
### "A Hard Day's Night"

Original script concept created to fill the role of Grey's Anatomy Season 2 Episode 1 while remaining an original story for Mercys Anatomy.

---

INT. MERCY GENERAL HOSPITAL - NIGHT - BEFORE DAWN

A cold, restless pre-dawn glow spills over the ER. The city hums beyond the windows, but inside the hospital, everything is sharp and electric.

We hear the alarm of a monitor. The camera tracks a fast-moving gurney.

DR. MERCY WELLS, 30s, brilliant, guarded, determined, pushes the gurney with steady focus, her eyes fixed on the patient. Her resident is behind her, nearly stumbling.

DR. ELLA MARSH, 20s, eager, overwhelmed, breathless.

ELLA
The patient is crashing.

MERCY
I know. That's why we run.

They slam through a set of double doors into the OR. Inside, the room is already moving.

SURGEON DR. RICHARD VALE, 50s, commanding, all calm precision.

RICHARD
We have an abdominal bleed. Monitor the pressure, get me a clamp.

MERCY
On it.

The team scrambles. Instruments rattle. The atmosphere is tight, no room for hesitation.

RICHARD
Mercy, can you handle the bleeding source?

MERCY
Yes.

She steps in without flinching. This is the place where she makes decisions fast and lives with the consequences.

CUT TO:

INT. MERCY GENERAL HOSPITAL - PRE-DAWN - RESIDENCY HALLWAY

A long hall. White walls, fluorescent lights. A handful of interns stand in knotty little clusters, trying to look awake and competent.

DR. CHLOE LANE, 20s, bright but over-prepared, checks a chart with too much intensity.

DR. LUCAS HART, 20s, charming and a little reckless, leans against a wall with a cup of coffee and a smirk.

DR. NOAH PRICE, 30s, controlled and cerebral, speaks quietly but with authority.

LUCAS
So what's the real story on the new chief? I heard he can make interns cry in under ten seconds.

NOAH
He can make surgeons cry in under ten seconds.

CHLOE
You should be studying.

LUCAS
I am studying. I'm studying the human condition through caffeine.

MERCY, still in scrub top, exits the OR, wiping blood from her forearms. She stops at the end of the hall and takes in the interns with a look that says she's already seen too much.

MERCY
If you have time to joke, you have time to learn.

The interns straighten instinctively, suddenly a little more alert.

MERCY
This hospital doesn't care about charm. It cares about whether you can keep a patient alive when things go wrong.

She passes them without another word.

LUCAS
Well. That's one way to start a day.

CHLOE
Maybe she's right.

NOAH
She's always right.

CUT TO:

INT. MERCY GENERAL HOSPITAL - ATTENDING'S LOUNGE - MORNING

A small break room with bad coffee and worse jokes. DR. KAT HAWKINS, 40s, sharp-edged, deeply competent, sits with a file in her lap, glaring at the ceiling like it owes her money.

DR. ALEX REED, 30s, polished and somehow emotionally unavailable, enters with a stack of charts.

ALEX
You're still here?

KAT
I never left. I got a crash call at 3:17 and then had to babysit two residents who both thought a bowel resection was a personality test.

ALEX
You sound proud.

KAT
Proud is a little too generous.

She flips a chart open and points.

KAT
This one is a mess. The patient was admitted three times before anyone noticed the infection. The intern missed the fever pattern. If I had known people could be this inattentive, I would've become a florist.

ALEX
You would be terrible at flowers.

KAT
I would be excellent at flowers. At least flowers don't lie.

ALEX
You say that like people do.

KAT
Most of them do. Try not to be one of them today.

Alex glances at her, maybe a little too long. Then he looks away.

ALEX
I wasn't planning on it.

A beat. Then they both look back to their work.

CUT TO:

INT. MERCY GENERAL HOSPITAL - HALLWAY OUTSIDE ICU - LATER

A nurse hustles past. A family waits outside a room. Noise swells and then settles.

MERCY and DR. JULIA FIELDS, 30s, soft-spoken but fearless, walk toward an elevator.

JULIA
I need a second opinion on Mr. Delgado. His blood pressure's dropping without any clear source.

MERCY
He's probably septic. We should look at the incision site.

JULIA
You always think sepsis.

MERCY
Because it's usually the answer.

They enter the elevator. The doors close.

Inside, the atmosphere shifts. The two women stand with a space between them, not awkward exactly, but loaded.

JULIA
You did a good job with the trauma patient last night.

MERCY
I got lucky.

JULIA
Lucky doesn't make a surgeon.

Mercy looks away, almost smiling.

MERCY
And you're going to stop complimenting me now because I will misinterpret it and I am trying to stay professional.

JULIA
You are impossible.

MERCY
I know.

The elevator dings. The doors open. They step out and continue toward the ICU.

CUT TO:

INT. MERCY GENERAL HOSPITAL - OPERATING ROOM 2 - DAY

A surgery underway. The room is clean, bright, controlled. RICHARD directs the operation with exacting calm.

MERCY stands at the table, suturing carefully. The patient is stable, but everyone's tense.

RICHARD
Keep the field clear.

MERCY
Yes, sir.

ELLA, hovering at the edge of the table, nearly steps in the wrong place and almost knocks a tray.

RICHARD
Pearl, don't crowd the table.

ELLA
I know.

RICHARD
Then act like it.

The room falls quiet for a moment, except for the beeping monitor.

MERCY glances up, sees the patient is slipping. Not enough to panic, but enough to recognize a problem.

MERCY
Pressure's dropping.

RICHARD
We have a bleed. Clamp.

The room erupts into motion as Mercy, Richard, and the team move in sync.

Mercy's hands are quick, steady, decisive.

MERCY
I got it.

RICHARD
Nice catch.

A beat. Then the room settles again.

INT. MERCY GENERAL HOSPITAL - HALLWAY - AFTERNOON

The interns gather near the nurses' station, pretending not to watch the attendings. Their notes are scattered. Their eyes are tired.

CHLOE
Did anyone see the case last night?

NOAH
I saw it.

LUCAS
I was in the parking lot with two coffees and a very questionable decision.

CHLOE
You came in late.

LUCAS
Fashionably late.

MERCY approaches, pocketing a chart. She looks from one to the next.

MERCY
You three, with me. We have a consult follow-up in room 12. Learn the process, not the shortcuts.

The interns exchange a glance. This is not an invitation. It's a summons.

CUT TO:

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM 12 - AFTERNOON

A patient lies in bed, pale and nervous. His family sits on the edge of the room, trying to make sense of the alarms and the white coats.

MERCY, CHLOE, NOAH, and LUCAS stand around the chart.

MERCY
The incision looks red. The wound's draining. He might have a post-op infection.

CHLOE
We should culture the fluid.

NOAH
And start broad-spectrum antibiotics.

LUCAS
Maybe ask if there are other symptoms.

Mercy nods in approval, surprisingly pleased.

MERCY
Good. Now ask the family why they waited four days before returning.

The interns all hesitate.

LUCAS
Because... they were scared?

MERCY
Exactly. And in a hospital, fear is information. You don't ignore it. You treat it like a symptom.

A beat.

MERCY
Now go in and talk to them.

The interns move as if they've been told to walk into the unknown. They do.

CUT TO:

INT. HOSPITAL ROOM 12 - CONTINUOUS

The family cluster together. The patient is anxious. The interns approach carefully.

LUCAS
Hi. I'm Dr. Hart. We were reviewing your chart and we want to understand what happened after the procedure.

CHLOE
We don't want you to feel brushed off.

NOAH
We're trying to decide the best next step.

The family speaks. The interns listen. The room softens.

This is the first time the interns actually hear the raw edge of medicine: fear, uncertainty, the way a patient's silence can tell you more than a chart ever could.

Mercy watches from the doorway, arms folded, expression unreadable.

Finally, the family looks relieved. The patient's breathing slows.

LUCAS
Thank you for telling us.

The interns step back, a little more human than they were before.

CUT TO:

INT. MERCY GENERAL HOSPITAL - ER TRAUMA BAY - EVENING

A siren wails in the distance. The trauma bay fills with motion. A nearly unconscious man is wheeled in. Blood streaks the gurney. The trauma team springs into action.

MERCY enters, taking the room in instantly.

MERCY
What happened?

NURSE
Hit by a car. Multiple injuries. He is bleeding internally.

MERCY
Let's go.

She works as if this is a familiar language: breathe, cut, clamp, hold. Her hands are precise. Her focus is absolute.

RICHARD
We need a decision fast.

MERCY
We go to the liver. Pressure's unstable. If we wait, he dies.

RICHARD
Do it.

The room pivots. Mercy moves without thinking. It is the kind of moment when life and death are just a few inches of tissue and the courage to act.

The surgery begins.

CUT TO:

INT. MERCY GENERAL HOSPITAL - NIGHT

The hospital is quieter now, but not calm. The fluorescent lights hum.

MERCY stands alone in a corridor, scrub cap in her hands, staring at a wall. She is exhausted but not done. She hears footsteps.

KAT
You always look like this after a tough OR?

MERCY
Like what?

KAT
Like the world is still spinning and you don't know if you're on the right side of it.

MERCY
I don't know if I am.

Kat studies her. Then leans against the wall beside her.

KAT
Then don't pretend to. It's not weakness.

MERCY
I don't think it's weakness.

KAT
Good. Then let's call it honest.

A beat. Mercy exhales, almost laughs.

MERCY
You say it like it's a strategy.

KAT
It is. The best one I've got.

Mercy nods. She looks toward the ICU, toward the future that waits beyond the doors.

MERCY
Tomorrow will be worse.

KAT
Probably.

MERCY
Then let's make it matter.

KAT
That's the spirit.

They step forward into the next shift.

CUT TO:

INT. MERCY GENERAL HOSPITAL - HALLWAY - DAWN

Morning light creeps in. The interns, a little worse for wear, walk together toward the next case list.

LUCAS
I thought I was going to throw up in room 12.

CHLOE
I thought I was going to throw up in the OR.

NOAH
I thought we were all going to throw up.

MERCY appears at the end of the hall, looking fresh, focused, and completely unsurprised by their collective consternation.

MERCY
Then you're learning.

They all look at each other, then at her.

LUCAS
Is that your version of a pep talk?

MERCY
No.

She pauses.

MERCY
It's my version of a warning.

She turns and keeps walking.

The interns stand there for a second and then follow.

FADE OUT.

---

END OF EPISODE

Note: This episode is an original script inspired by the energy, structure, and high-stakes medical drama of Grey's Anatomy Season 2 Episode 1, adapted into the Mercys Anatomy universe.
