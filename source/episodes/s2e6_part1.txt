H: INT. LIRR TRAIN - CAR 4 - MORNING
T: THURSDAY, OCTOBER 20, 2005 - 7:46 AM
A: A packed rush-hour Long Island Rail Road train from Huntington, westbound toward Penn Station on the Main Line. Commuters with coffee, newspapers, iPods. The Daily News headline in one man's hands: "SADDAM ON TRIAL." On another: "WILMA: STRONGEST ATLANTIC HURRICANE EVER."
A: Standing in the vestibule, holding the same vertical steel pole: LORRAINE FULLER (41), a Bloomingdale's buyer in a camel coat, with a cell phone at her ear; and DWAYNE RICKS (38), a Nassau County court officer in uniform, reading a paperback.
C: LORRAINE| (into the phone, laughing)| Keith. Keith. (beat) I'm not saying it on a train. (beat) Because I'm forty-one and you've had four years to say it first. (beat) Tonight. Dinner. I'll say it at dinner.
A: She hangs up, smiling. She catches Dwayne smiling at the paperback. He glances up.
C: DWAYNE| (apologetic)| Sorry. It's a small train.
C: LORRAINE| (laughing)| Four years. I've never said it. He's never said it. (beat) We're both too proud.
C: DWAYNE| (beat)| My wife and I said it on the second date. (beat) Twelve years. Three kids. (beat) Say it, lady. Life's short.
C: LORRAINE| (beat)| Tonight.
A: The train rocks through Woodside. Out the window, the Manhattan skyline appears through the October haze.
C: MARC (V.O.)| They say you never see the big ones coming.
A: A shriek of metal. Brakes. The car lurches violently. Lorraine and Dwayne slam into each other against the pole.
C: MARC (V.O.)| That's not true. (beat) You always see them coming. You just don't know what you're looking at.
A: CRASH. Darkness.

Natasha (47), in her white coat with an orange "INCIDENT COMMANDER" vest over it, stands on the loading platform with a megaphone and a clipboard. Bernie (55) beside her with a radio. Robert, Elxa, Graves, Crump, Lowery and Clayton gather below.

C: NATASHA| (crisp)| Red tags: Pit, Trauma One through Six, with Dr. Elxa Bullock and Dr. Robert Bullock. (beat) Neuro: Dr. Graves, Trauma Seven and OR Six. (beat) OB: Dr. Hartwell. H: INT. PELL PAVILION - THE PIT - TRAUMA 1 - DAY
T: THURSDAY, OCTOBER 20, 2005 - 8:21 AM
A: The doors bang open. Four paramedics and two FDNY firefighters wheel in a strange, terrible double load: two stretchers lashed side by side. Lying on them, face to face, as if embracing: Lorraine Fuller and Dwayne Ricks. A single length of steel pole, cut off with a rescue saw at both ends, runs through both of their bodies at the waist.
A: Everyone in the Pit stops for half a second.
C: TEE ROLLINS| (pushing, shouting)| Two victims, one pole! Vestibule handrail, sheared at the derailment! Through the female's left flank and out her right side, and into the male's right lower abdomen! (beat) Female's Lorraine Fuller, forty-one. Male's Dwayne Ricks, thirty-eight. (beat) Both awake. Both talking. FDNY cut the pole at the car so we could move them together.
A: Elxa (28), twenty-four weeks pregnant, and Robert (49) move in. Marc, just arrived, out of breath, is right behind them. Raven (25) and Harmony (26) follow.
C: ELXA| Nobody touches the pole. Nobody. (beat) Two of everything. Two monitors. Two lines each. Type and cross ten units each.
A: Lorraine, her face inches from Dwayne's, is gray and shaking but talking.
C: LORRAINE| (through her teeth)| We have to stop meeting like this.
C: DWAYNE| (gasping, almost laughing)| Lady, you said tonight.
C: ROBERT| (to Marc)| Dr. Bullock. Junior. You're on Mrs. Fuller. Dr. Ellison, you're on Mr. Ricks. You don't leave your patient. You don't let go of anything until I say so. (beat) CT, both of them together, in four minutes.
C: MARC| (moving to Lorraine's side)| Ms. Fuller. I'm Dr. Bullock. I'm staying with you.
C: LORRAINE| (looking up at him)| Bullock. (beat) Like the Chief? (beat) I read about her. Bloomingdale's sponsored a Donohue gala.
C: MARC| (steady)| Bullock's a common name, Ms. Fuller.
C: LORRAINE| (beat, a faint smile)| Liar.

H: EXT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - AMBULANCE BAY - DAY
T: THURSDAY, OCTOBER 20, 2005 - 8:29 AM
A: A third wave of ambulances. A Red Cross triage tent is half up. Amond, in a hoodie and jeans with his Queens Grace ID clipped on, walks into the middle of the bay and picks up a box of triage tags off a cart.
A: Robert, coming out to grab a stretcher, stops dead.
C: ROBERT| Dr. Baker. You're on medical leave.
C: AMOND| (not looking up, sorting tags)| I'm not scrubbing. I'm not lifting. I'm not touching a sterile field. (beat) I'm reading people. (beat) It's a mass casualty, sir. You need someone who reads people fast.
A: Natasha comes down the ramp with her megaphone. She sees Amond. She sees Robert's face.
C: NATASHA| (beat)| Robert. All hands. (beat) He doesn't scrub. He tags.
C: ROBERT| (beat)| Tasha, he had six units sixteen days ago.
C: NATASHA| (beat)| And today he's the best triage officer I'm going to get in the next ninety seconds. (to Amond) Dr. Baker. If you get dizzy, you sit. If you sit, I'll know.
C: AMOND| (already moving toward the next ambulance)| Yes, ma'am.
A: He reaches the first stretcher, a moaning man in a bloody suit. Amond looks at him for exactly four seconds: the color, the breathing, the eyes.
C: AMOND| (writing, ripping off a tag)| Yellow. Tibia. Breathing fine. Ortho. (to the next) Red. Tension physiology, no breath sounds on the left. Pit, now. (to the next) Green. Lacerations, walking, talking, angry. Clinic. (to the next, softer) Black.
