H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - SECURITY OFFICE - NIGHT
T: MONDAY, OCTOBER 3, 2005 - 6:08 PM

C: NATASHA| Sit, Ray.

C: BIG RAY| (quietly)| Dr. Whitlow sent a request to my supervisor at two o'clock. Copies of the loading dock log and the camera footage for August ninth, from four to six a.m. (beat) He came down himself at four. Asked me, very friendly, who I escorted that morning.
C: NATASHA| (beat)| And what did you tell him?
C: BIG RAY| That I escort a lot of people, Chief. Some of them don't like their names on paper. (beat) He smiled. He said he'd see the footage.
C: NATASHA| (beat)| The footage.
C: BIG RAY| (beat)| The loading dock camera's been broken since July. Work order's on file. I've asked them to fix it four times. (beat) I didn't break it, Chief. I'm saying that now, so you never have to wonder.
C: NATASHA| (quietly)| I never would, Ray.
C: BIG RAY| (beat)| And Chief. If they ever put me under oath, about anything, I'll tell the truth. (beat) I was twenty years NYPD. I don't lie under oath.

H: INT. BROOKLYN HEIGHTS - DR. GREENIDGE'S OFFICE - NIGHT
T: MONDAY, OCTOBER 3, 2005 - 6:34 PM
A: A brownstone parlor-floor office on Montague Street: two armchairs, a sofa, a fern, a window onto the Promenade and the lights of Lower Manhattan. DR. PAULETTE GREENIDGE (50), locs pulled back, cardigan, a voice like warm tea, sits in one armchair. Esther sits at one end of the sofa. Clayton at the other. A careful three feet between them.
C: DR. GREENIDGE| Session seven. (beat) Last Thursday, Esther, you said there was a question you'd been afraid to ask. (beat) Are you ready to ask it?
A: Esther looks at her hands. Then at Clayton.
C: ESTHER| (quietly)| February eleventh. (beat) Was it one night?
A: Clayton's face doesn't move. But he takes a long time. Too long.
C: DR. GREENIDGE| (gently)| Clayton.
C: CLAYTON| (finally, evenly)| No.
A: Esther closes her eyes.
C: CLAYTON| (beat)| It started on New Year's Eve. At the party in Milton. (beat) It ended on February eleventh, because you came home early. (beat) Six weeks.
C: ESTHER| (barely)| Six weeks.
C: CLAYTON| (looking at her)| I could have said "one night." You'd have believed me. (beat) I've had twenty-two days to say "one night." (beat) I'm not going to lie to you in the room where we agreed not to lie.
C: ESTHER| (opening her eyes, shaking)| Six weeks of coming home to me. Of sleeping next to me. Of eating my mother's Sunday dinners with Malcolm across the table.
C: CLAYTON| (beat)| Yes.
A: Silence. Dr. Greenidge lets it sit.
C: DR. GREENIDGE| (softly)| Esther. What are you feeling right now? One word.
C: ESTHER| (beat)| Stupid.
C: DR. GREENIDGE| (beat)| And Clayton?
C: CLAYTON| (beat, the polish gone)| Ashamed.
C: DR. GREENIDGE| (gently)| Those are the first two true words either of you has said to the other in seven sessions. (beat) We'll stop there tonight.

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - SURGICAL INTENSIVE CARE UNIT - BED 3 - NIGHT
T: MONDAY, OCTOBER 3, 2005 - 6:44 PM
A: Tyrell, awake, propped up. Lula sits beside him. Kordell sits on the end of the bed with a bag of Doritos. Raven checks the drains.

C: TYRELL| (to Raven)| The doctor with the suction said go anyway.

H: EXT. BROOKLYN HEIGHTS - MONTAGUE STREET - NIGHT
T: MONDAY, OCTOBER 3, 2005 - 7:22 PM
A: The sidewalk outside the brownstone. Esther walks fast toward the subway at Court Street. Clayton catches up and walks beside her, not touching.
C: CLAYTON| Es.
C: ESTHER| (not stopping)| Don't.
C: CLAYTON| I told you the truth.
C: ESTHER| (stopping, rounding on him)| You told me the truth on day twenty-two, Clayton. You've been sitting across from me for twenty-two days, letting me think it was one stupid night.
C: CLAYTON| (beat, quietly)| Yes. (beat) And how many nights did you sit across from him?
A: That lands like a slap. Esther stares at him.
C: CLAYTON| (gently, which is worse)| I'm not saying it to hurt you. I'm saying it because it's true. (beat) We're the same kind of coward, Es. That's what's so terrible about it. (beat) We both thought if we waited long enough, the truth would get easier to say.
A: Esther looks at him for a long moment. Then she turns and walks down the subway stairs alone. Clayton stands on the sidewalk under the streetlight. He doesn't follow.

H: INT. MONTY'S ON JACKSON - BAR - NIGHT
T: MONDAY, OCTOBER 3, 2005 - 7:58 PM
A: A Monday crowd. The Yankees-Angels preview on TV, the division series opening tomorrow. In the corner booth: Marc, Raven, Arianna and Amond, who has a club soda and the mason jar of fig preserves on the table in front of him like a trophy.
C: RAVEN| (pointing at the jar)| You brought preserves to a bar.
C: AMOND| A sixty-nine-year-old woman who chopped through a roof gave them to me. I'm not leaving them in a locker.
C: ARIANNA| (taking a spoon from a cup of coffee stirrers and digging in)| They're good.
C: AMOND| (snatching the jar back)| Cummings.
A: Monty (42) brings a round.
C: MONTY| Doc. (to Amond) Your blood count come back?
C: AMOND| (surprised)| How do you know about my...
C: MONTY| (tapping his ear)| Bar.
C: AMOND| (beat)| Twelve point one. (beat) It's within normal limits.
C: MARC| (frowning)| What was it in July?
C: AMOND| (beat)| Fourteen point six.
A: Marc and Arianna exchange a look. Amond pointedly drinks his club soda.
A: A WOMAN at the bar, DANIELLA (27), pretty, in a Fordham Law sweatshirt, catches Marc's eye and smiles. She comes over.
C: DANIELLA| (to Marc)| You're the doctor from the blog. Dr. X.
C: MARC| (polite)| I'm a doctor. Blogs are fiction.
C: DANIELLA| (smiling)| Can I buy Fiction a drink?
A: Raven and Arianna both lean in, very interested. Marc smiles at Daniella, a real smile, kind.
C: MARC| (gently)| That's really nice of you. (beat) But I'm not in a good place to be anybody's anything right now. (beat) You'd deserve better than half my attention.
C: DANIELLA| (beat, a little surprised, then nodding)| That's the nicest no I've ever gotten. (beat) Good luck, Fiction.

C: ARIANNA| (to Raven, low)| He's going to be a monk. We're going to lose him to a monastery.
C: RAVEN| (to Arianna, low, grinning)| You'd visit.
C: ARIANNA| (beat, grinning back despite herself)| I'd bring beer.
A: Their eyes hold for half a second longer than usual. Then they both look away. Amond, who notices everything, notices that.

H: INT. NATASHA AND ROBERT'S BROWNSTONE - RIVERSIDE DRIVE - KITCHEN - NIGHT
T: MONDAY, OCTOBER 3, 2005 - 8:34 PM

C: NATASHA| Ray told me the loading dock camera's been broken since July. Work order on file. (beat) Horace won't find anything.
C: ROBERT| (beat, turning off the burner)| Tasha.
C: NATASHA| What?

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - LABOR AND DELIVERY - NURSES' STATION - NIGHT

C: WHITLOW| Remarkable woman. Remarkable family. (beat) You must have known the Donohues, in Boston. Brookline, Milton. It's a small world, Black Boston.
C: CLAYTON| (beat, carefully)| Everyone knows of the Donohues, Dr. Whitlow.
C: WHITLOW| (smiling)| Of course. (beat) I only ask because I'm putting together a little history for the board. Of the Foundation and this hospital. Families, connections. Who's related to whom. (beat) I'd value your perspective sometime. Over lunch. A newcomer sees things the rest of us are too close to notice.
A: He hands Clayton a business card. Clayton looks at it. Then at Whitlow.
C: CLAYTON| (pleasantly)| I'm a fetal surgeon, Dr. Whitlow. (beat) I only notice things that are still inside.
C: WHITLOW| (laughing warmly)| Lunch, then. (beat) Whenever you like.
A: Whitlow walks away down the corridor. Clayton turns the card over in his fingers. Then he puts it in his breast pocket. Not in the trash. In his pocket.
