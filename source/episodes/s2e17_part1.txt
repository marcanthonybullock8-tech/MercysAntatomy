X: COLD OPEN

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - OR 1 - NIGHT
T: SUNDAY, FEBRUARY 5, 2006 - 6:22 PM
A: Silence, except for the soft rhythmic squeeze of Mabry's hand on the ventilation bag. Marc (27) kneels on the step stool. Burrell (44), in his full bomb suit, crouches beside him with the open containment pot. Lowery (59) holds the wound edges open with his gloved hands.
C: BURRELL| (through the helmet)| One.
A: Marc lifts. Slowly. His hand rises out of Reggie's body, his fingers wrapped around the rusted olive-drab shell. An inch. Two. It comes free of the wound, dark with blood and old mud. He holds it straight, perfectly level, in the air above the open abdomen.
C: MARC (V.O.)| They teach you in medical school that the body has its own wisdom.
A: Burrell raises both gloved hands under Marc's. Marc lowers the shell into Burrell's palms. For a second, both of them hold it. Then Marc lets go.
C: BURRELL| (very quietly)| I have it, Doctor. (beat) Let go.
A: Burrell lowers the shell into the containment pot. He closes the lid with a heavy click and turns two latches.
C: MARC (V.O.)| That before the mind knows, the body already does.

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - OR 1 - NIGHT
T: SUNDAY, FEBRUARY 5, 2006 - 6:24 PM
A: Burrell stands with the heavy pot against his chest. He looks at Marc through the face shield. Marc's hand is still in the air, empty, shaking now for the first time in four hours.
C: BURRELL| (beat)| You did good, Dr. Bullock. (beat) Go wash your hands.
C: MARC| (beat, barely)| Sergeant.
C: BURRELL| (beat)| Find yourself a Tuesday, son.
A: He turns and walks to the east door of the operating room, the one that leads to the service corridor. A police officer holds it open for him. He walks through. The door swings shut.
C: MARC (V.O.)| A racing heart. A hand that won't stop shaking.
A: Lowery looks down at the empty space in Reggie's abdomen where the shell was.
C: LOWERY| (beat, very quietly)| Edwin. (beat) Let's close this man up.
C: MABRY| (beat, still bagging)| Gladly, Abraham.
C: MARC (V.O.)| The body trying to tell you something you're not ready to hear.
X: END OF COLD OPEN

X: ACT ONE

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - STAIRWELL C - NIGHT
T: SUNDAY, FEBRUARY 5, 2006 - 6:28 PM
A: The emergency lights. Elxa (28) on the landing against the wall, on Harmony's white coat, red-faced, pushing with a deep, raw groan. Harmony (26) kneels in front of her, gloved, steady. The cell phone lies on the step, on speaker.
C: DR. HAMPTON (V.O.)| (on the phone)| ...when you see the head, Harmony, you don't pull. (beat) You guide. (beat) Let her do the work.
C: HARMONY| (calm)| I see the head. (beat) Dr. Bullock, I see the head. (beat) He has hair. (beat) So much hair.
C: ELXA| (collapsing back between pushes, gasping)| Of course he does. (beat) He's a Donohue.
A: A sound from far away, down through the building: the distant roar of a crowd from a TV somewhere. The kickoff.
C: ELXA| (beat, half laughing, half crying)| Oh, God. (beat) The Super Bowl. (beat) Russell's going to miss the kickoff.
C: HARMONY| (beat, gently)| Russell's going to miss a lot of things tonight. (beat) Not this one. (beat) You'll tell him.
C: DR. HAMPTON (V.O.)| (on the phone)| Next contraction, Elxa. (beat) Chin to your chest.

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - SERVICE CORRIDOR - OLD WING - NIGHT
T: SUNDAY, FEBRUARY 5, 2006 - 6:29 PM
A: A long, narrow concrete corridor with pipes along the ceiling and old fluorescent fixtures, empty, leading to the loading dock doors at the far end. Burrell walks down the middle of it alone, slowly, the containment pot held against his chest in both arms. His breath fogs the inside of his face shield. Two officers stand at the far end by the loading dock, holding the doors.
A: One of the fluorescent tubes overhead flickers. Buzzes. Flickers. Burrell glances up at it and keeps walking.
C: BURRELL| (to himself, softly)| Tuesday. (beat) Pink. (beat) Tuesday.
A: Forty feet to go. Thirty-five.

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - OR 1 - SCRUB ROOM - NIGHT
T: SUNDAY, FEBRUARY 5, 2006 - 6:30 PM
A: Marc stands at the long steel scrub sink, staring at his hands under the running water. Blood and rust swirl down the drain. His hands are shaking badly now. He can't make them stop. Through the small window in the door, he can see down the service corridor: the white bulk of Burrell's suit, walking away toward the loading dock.
A: Outside the other door, Natasha (47) and Robert (49) wait behind the police line. Natasha sees Marc through the glass and presses her hand flat to it again. Marc looks up and sees her.
C: MARC| (barely, through the glass, though she can't hear)| Mama.
A: He looks back through the window down the corridor. Burrell is twenty feet from the loading dock. The flickering fluorescent tube above him buzzes loudly and goes dark.

A: A blinding white flash. A sound like the end of the world.

X: ACT TWO

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - OR 1 - SCRUB ROOM - NIGHT
T: SUNDAY, FEBRUARY 5, 2006 - 6:31 PM
A: Silence. High-pitched ringing. Dust and smoke. The small window in the scrub-room door has cracked into a spiderweb. Marc lies on the floor under the steel sink, where the blast threw him. The water is still running. Blood trickles from a cut above his left eyebrow.
A: He can't hear anything. He sees, as if underwater: ceiling tiles drifting down; a fire alarm strobe flashing; the door to the corridor bursting open; his father running through it, his mouth open, shouting something Marc can't hear.
A: Robert drops to his knees beside his son and takes his face in both hands.
C: ROBERT| (shouting, muffled, far away)| Marc! (beat) Marc! (beat) Look at me!
A: Marc looks at him. Robert's hands move over his skull, his neck, his chest, his arms, a surgeon's hands checking for injuries. Natasha is right behind him. She kneels on the other side, in her good coat, in the dust.
C: MARC| (dazed, too loud, unable to hear his own voice)| I can't hear you.
C: NATASHA| (exaggerating every word so he can read her lips)| You're alive.
A: Marc stares at her. Then he looks past her, through the shattered window, down the corridor full of smoke.
C: MARC| (beat)| Where's the sergeant?

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - STAIRWELL C - NIGHT
T: SUNDAY, FEBRUARY 5, 2006 - 6:32 PM
A: The stairwell shudders. Dust sifts down from the ceiling. The emergency lights flicker and come back. Harmony throws herself forward over Elxa instinctively, covering her and the baby that isn't born yet. The phone on the step crackles.
C: DR. HAMPTON (V.O.)| (on the phone, frantic)| Harmony! (beat) Harmony, what was that? (beat) We heard it out here! (beat) The whole street heard it!
A: Harmony slowly lifts her head. Elxa stares up at the ceiling, wide-eyed, her breath coming in gasps.
C: ELXA| (beat, barely)| That was the bomb.
C: HARMONY| (beat, very quietly)| I know.
C: ELXA| (beat)| My brother's down there. (beat) Marc is down there.
A: Harmony looks at Elxa. She knows. Everyone in the building who heard the overhead page and saw the police in the old wing knows. She says nothing about it. She puts both hands back in position.
C: HARMONY| (beat, steady)| Dr. Bullock. (beat) Look at me. (beat) Not at the ceiling. (beat) At me. (beat) Your son is coming. (beat) Right now. (beat) Next contraction. (beat) Push.

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - SERVICE CORRIDOR - OLD WING - NIGHT
T: SUNDAY, FEBRUARY 5, 2006 - 6:35 PM
A: Smoke. Twisted pipe. The overhead lights are gone; flashlight beams cut through the haze. The loading dock doors at the far end hang off their hinges. Three bomb squad officers move through the debris toward something on the floor near the end of the corridor. Marc stumbles into the corridor behind them, his father trying to hold him back by the arm.
C: ROBERT| (shouting)| Marc, no!
A: Marc pulls free. An officer turns and catches him by the shoulders. Over the officer's shoulder, in the flashlight beams, Marc sees the white bulk of the bomb suit lying still on the concrete, and the two officers kneeling beside it. One of them takes off his own helmet and bows his head.
A: Marc stops struggling. The officer holding him looks into his face.
C: OFFICER| (gently, his mouth moving clearly)| He's gone, Doc. (beat) The pot didn't hold. (beat) He's gone.
A: Marc's knees buckle. The officer holds him up. Robert reaches him and takes his son's weight from the officer. Marc stands in his father's arms in the smoke and stares at the man on the floor who told him to find a Tuesday.
C: MARC| (barely, unable to hear himself)| He said "Tuesday."

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - STAIRWELL C - NIGHT
T: SUNDAY, FEBRUARY 5, 2006 - 6:44 PM
A: Elxa bears down with a scream that echoes up four flights of concrete. Harmony kneels, her hands cradling the emerging head. The face turns. A thick, bluish loop of umbilical cord is wrapped around the baby's neck.
C: HARMONY| (beat, very calm, to the phone)| Dr. Hampton, the cord's around his neck.
C: DR. HAMPTON (V.O.)| (instantly, steady)| Is it loose enough to slip over his head?
C: HARMONY| (feeling it with one finger)| I think so.
C: DR. HAMPTON (V.O.)| Then slip it over his head, honey. (beat) Gently. (beat) Now.
A: Harmony hooks one gloved finger under the cord and slides it over the baby's head. It comes free.
C: HARMONY| (beat)| It's off.
C: DR. HAMPTON (V.O.)| (beat)| Then let her push.
C: HARMONY| (beat, to Elxa)| Push, Elxa. (beat) Push.
A: Elxa pushes. One shoulder, then the other. And then, all at once, slippery and gray and furious, the baby slides into Harmony's hands. Silence. One second. Two. Harmony turns him over, rubs his back hard with her palm.
A: He takes a breath. Then he screams. A huge, outraged, wonderful scream that fills the stairwell and goes up four flights and down three.
C: HARMONY| (sobbing and laughing at once)| He's here. (beat) He's here, Elxa. (beat) He's a boy. (beat) He's a big, loud boy.
C: DR. HAMPTON (V.O.)| (on the phone, crying)| Time, Harmony. (beat) What time is it?
A: Harmony glances at her watch through her tears.
C: HARMONY| (beat)| Six forty-four p.m.
X: END OF ACT TWO
