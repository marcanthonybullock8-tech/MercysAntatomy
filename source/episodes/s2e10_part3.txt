H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - BOARDROOM - NIGHT
T: THURSDAY, DECEMBER 1, 2005 - 5:31 PM
A: The long mahogany table. Portraits of past Chiefs on the walls. Natasha at one side with Bassett and Bernie. Whitlow across from her, immaculate, his hands folded. The CEO, the Chief Nursing Officer and the program director. At the head: Dr. Albritton, Mayfield and Haskins.
C: DR. ALBRITTON| (reading from her summary)| This for-cause survey was conducted in response to a complaint alleging conflicts of interest in the supervision and evaluation of a resident physician related to hospital leadership. (beat) We conducted three patient tracers, interviewed nineteen staff members, and reviewed one hundred and nine surgical cases.
A: Whitlow's eyes don't leave her.
C: DR. ALBRITTON| (beat)| The complaint is not substantiated. (beat) Supervision and evaluation of the resident in question were appropriate, well documented, and, in the surveyors' judgment, exemplary. (beat) The Department of Surgery's November ninth service firewall is noted as a leading practice. (beat) We also observed full compliance with the Universal Protocol, including a model time-out, in the operating room this morning.
A: Natasha doesn't move. Bernie, beside her, closes her eyes.
C: DR. ALBRITTON| (turning a page)| However. (beat) A tracer on the Department of Medicine identified a patient who received duplicate digoxin therapy for approximately forty-eight hours, due to the absence of a medication reconciliation process. (beat) National Patient Safety Goal eight. (beat) The toxicity was identified by a surgical intern on consult, and the patient has recovered. (beat) This is a Requirement for Improvement for the Department of Medicine. (beat) Evidence of Standards Compliance is due within forty-five days.
A: Every head at the table turns, very slowly, toward Whitlow.
C: WHITLOW| (evenly)| The Department of Medicine will comply in full, Dr. Albritton.
C: DR. ALBRITTON| (beat, looking at him over her glasses)| I'm sure it will, Dr. Whitlow. (beat) Complaints often take us places the complainant didn't expect. (beat) That's rather the point of having us come.
A: She closes her folder.

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - EXECUTIVE CORRIDOR - NIGHT
T: THURSDAY, DECEMBER 1, 2005 - 5:54 PM
A: The meeting is breaking up. Whitlow walks fast down the carpeted hall. Natasha comes out of the boardroom behind him.
C: NATASHA| Horace.
A: He stops and turns.
C: WHITLOW| (low, controlled)| You pointed them at Medicine.
C: NATASHA| (beat)| I never said the word "Medicine" in front of any of them. Ask Dr. Albritton. (beat) I opened every door in this hospital, Horace. (beat) You're the one who invited them in.
C: WHITLOW| (beat)| Your son's roommate. (beat) Baker. (beat) Of all the interns in this building.
C: NATASHA| (beat)| Amond Baker caught a fatal medication error on your floor that your own residents missed for two days. (beat) You should send him a fruit basket.
A: Whitlow looks at her. For the first time in six months, he has nothing to say.
C: NATASHA| (beat, quieter)| You wanted them to wonder about my son, Horace. (beat) Now they're going to wonder about you. (beat) That isn't revenge. (beat) That's just the truth, catching up.
A: She walks past him. He stands alone in the long corridor under the portraits of the past Chiefs.

H: INT. QUEENS GRACE HOSPITAL - ELEVATOR - NIGHT
T: THURSDAY, DECEMBER 1, 2005 - 6:03 PM
A: Marc, in his coat, steps into the elevator. Dr. Albritton is already there with her rolling briefcase. The doors close. A long beat.
C: MARC| (carefully)| Dr. Albritton.
C: DR. ALBRITTON| (eyes on the numbers)| Dr. Bullock. (beat) I'm not permitted to discuss the findings with you.
C: MARC| (beat)| I understand.
A: The elevator descends. Four. Three.
C: DR. ALBRITTON| (beat, still looking at the numbers)| In 1971, I was the only Black woman in my class at Meharry who wanted to be a surgeon. (beat) A professor told me I'd be better suited to pediatrics. "Gentler hands." (beat) I became an internist instead, out of spite, and I've regretted it for thirty years.
A: Marc looks at her.
C: DR. ALBRITTON| (beat)| Your hands are your own, Dr. Bullock. (beat) Don't let anyone, a professor, a rival or a grandfather, tell you otherwise.
A: The doors open on the lobby. She rolls her briefcase out and doesn't look back.

H: INT. CLAYTON'S SUBLET - BROOKLYN HEIGHTS - LIVING ROOM - NIGHT
T: THURSDAY, DECEMBER 1, 2005 - 7:12 PM
A: Clayton cooks pasta in the galley kitchen. Esther sits on the sofa with her phone in her lap. More of her things are here now: a second box, her books on the shelf, her white coat on the hook beside his.
C: ESTHER| (beat)| I want to go home for Christmas. (beat) To Mattapan. (beat) Both of us.
C: CLAYTON| (stirring, carefully)| Okay.
C: ESTHER| (beat)| And I'm going to ask my mother to invite Malcolm.
A: Clayton stops stirring. He turns.
C: CLAYTON| (beat)| Esther.
C: ESTHER| (beat)| A boy from Roxbury ate at my mother's table for twenty-seven years because his own parents wouldn't feed him. (beat) I saw a man today who ate a hundred and seventy-four wings to pay his mother's light bill. (beat) And I thought, Malcolm was always eating to pay for something. (beat) I'm not forgiving him. (beat) I'm just not going to be the reason there's an empty chair.
C: CLAYTON| (beat, barely)| Is that too much?
C: ESTHER| (beat)| Probably. (beat) Dr. Greenidge will have a field day. (beat) Ask me again on Christmas Eve.
A: Clayton turns back to the stove. His hand is shaking slightly on the spoon.

H: INT. THE HOUSE ON 49TH AVENUE - KITCHEN - NIGHT
T: THURSDAY, DECEMBER 1, 2005 - 7:43 PM
A: Raven comes in from the cold, exhausted. Marc, Amond and Arianna sit at the kitchen table, looking suspiciously innocent. On the counter: a stack of mail. On top, a small square envelope in a careful hand she now recognizes. "L. ELLISON, MLK JR. BLVD., SAVANNAH, GA."
A: Raven picks it up. She looks at the others. Then she opens it, right there in the kitchen, the first letter she hasn't put in the shoebox. A birthday card. A cartoon cake.
C: RAVEN| (reading aloud, her voice catching)| "Happy 26th birthday, my Raven. (beat) Twenty-six years ago Saturday, I held you in Memorial Hospital and promised I'd be better. (beat) I wasn't, for a long time. (beat) I am now. (beat) If it's not too much, I'd like to see you. Christmas, or whenever you say. (beat) If it's too much, I understand. (beat) Mama."
A: Silence in the kitchen. Raven sets the card down on the counter.
C: MARC| (beat, carefully)| Rave. (beat) I have to tell you something. (beat) Today at lunch, I almost bought her a plane ticket. (beat) For Saturday. (beat) A surprise.
A: Raven turns to look at him.
C: AMOND| (dry)| I stopped him.
C: MARC| (beat)| Amond stopped me. (beat) Because it's yours. (beat) I'm sorry I even thought it.
A: Raven looks at Marc for a long moment. Then she crosses the kitchen and hugs him hard.
C: RAVEN| (into his shoulder)| Thank you for not. (beat) And thank you for almost.
C: ARIANNA| (beat)| So what are you going to tell her?
A: Raven picks up a pen from the counter, turns the card over and writes one word on the back of an envelope. She holds it up. "CHRISTMAS."

H: INT. CAMILLE'S APARTMENT - JACKSON HEIGHTS - BEDROOM - NIGHT
T: THURSDAY, DECEMBER 1, 2005 - 9:26 PM
A: The bedroom, lit by a single lamp and the streetlight through the blinds. The cast-iron pot sits clean on the stove in the next room. Camille and Harmony sit on the edge of the bed, close, their shoulders touching.
C: CAMILLE| (beat)| You can still go home, Choir. (beat) Today was a lot.
C: HARMONY| (beat)| Today was much too much.
C: CAMILLE| (beat, quietly)| Yeah.
C: HARMONY| (beat, turning to her)| So let's have one thing tonight that's just enough.
A: Camille looks at her. Then she reaches over and turns off the lamp. In the dark, the sound of two women laughing softly at nothing. Then not laughing.

H: INT. ELXA AND RUSSELL'S HOUSE - ST. ALBANS - LIVING ROOM - NIGHT
T: THURSDAY, DECEMBER 1, 2005 - 10:14 PM
A: A cozy two-family house. Stacks of graded chemistry labs on the coffee table. Elxa, in pajamas, sits on an enormous purple birthing ball, bouncing gently, eyes closed. Russell, on the floor beside her, times her breathing with his wristwatch like a man running a titration.
C: RUSSELL| (counting)| In, two, three, four. (beat) Out, two, three, four. (beat) Elxa. (beat) You're doing it wrong.
C: ELXA| (eyes still closed)| I'm a surgeon, Russell.
C: RUSSELL| (beat)| And I'm the chemistry teacher, so I know a reaction when I see one. (beat) You're holding your breath at the top.
A: Elxa opens her eyes. She breathes out. All the way. Something in her shoulders lets go.
C: ELXA| (beat)| The Joint Commission cleared Marc today. (beat) Mama called. (beat) She cried. On the phone. To me. (beat) She hasn't cried on the phone to me since my wedding.
C: RUSSELL| (beat, putting a hand on her belly)| And this one?
C: ELXA| (beat)| Thirty weeks. (beat) Kicking like he wants out.
C: RUSSELL| (beat)| Tell him February. (beat) Super Bowl. (beat) We'll have the TV on.
C: ELXA| (beat, laughing)| You're not watching football while I'm in labor.
C: RUSSELL| (beat)| Watch me.

H: INT. MTA QUEENS VILLAGE DEPOT - QUEENS VILLAGE - NIGHT
T: THURSDAY, DECEMBER 1, 2005 - 11:14 PM
A: A cavernous bus depot, rows of blue-and-white buses under the sodium lights. Howard Divine's Q4 pulls into its berth. He shuts off the engine. The bus is empty. He walks to the first seat behind his own and unties the black ribbon. He folds it carefully, the way you fold a flag.
A: He coughs. Deep, wet, long. He sits down in the seat for a moment, catching his breath, the folded ribbon in his hands.
C: MARC (V.O.)| A surgeon learns that there's such a thing as too much.
A: Howard puts the ribbon in his breast pocket, over his heart. He gets up and walks out of the depot into the cold.

H: EXT. THE HOUSE ON 49TH AVENUE - STOOP - NIGHT
T: THURSDAY, DECEMBER 1, 2005 - 11:58 PM
A: Marc on the stoop, his breath visible in the cold. Upstairs, Raven's light is on. Her shadow on the blind holds the birthday card. Across the river, the Manhattan skyline.

C: MARC (V.O.)| But nobody tells you about the other kind. (beat) Too much love. Too much hope. Too much history in one house.

C: MARC (V.O.)| Fifty years ago tonight, a seamstress in Montgomery decided that too much was too much, and sat down. (beat) And that turned out to be just enough.
